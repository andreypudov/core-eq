#+TITLE: CoreEQ 1.9 — Open the preset format
#+AUTHOR: Andrey Pudov
#+DATE: <2026-09-30 Wed>
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

The most requested feature, by a clear margin, was loading an AutoEQ or
EqualizerAPO profile — asked for as AutoEQ, EqualizerAPO, Wavelet, and “EQ txt
files”, which are all the same file. 1.9 reads it and writes it. It also answers
the screen recordings that came out with an echo, and a sample rate change that
left the sound chopped until CoreEQ was restarted.

* New

- *Import and export.* EqualizerAPO and AutoEQ text, and CoreEQ's own =.coreeq=,
  from the =+= under the preset list, by pasting with =⌘V=, or by dropping a file
  on the list. Every import shows what it found first, including anything
  dropped to fit sixteen filters, adjusted to fit CoreEQ's limits, or skipped.
  UTF-8, UTF-16, and Windows-1252 files all read. Export is on a preset's
  context menu, and exporting the preset you are listening to writes what you
  hear. A real AutoEQ profile, the Sennheiser HD 600, matches the response
  AutoEQ publishes for it within 0.064 dB, and what CoreEQ writes reloads
  identically. Contributed by Sebastian Ullrich.
- *An AutoEQ guide.* The AutoEQ button beside the =+= walks through getting a
  correction for your headphones from autoeq.app.
- *Pause while recording system audio.* A screen recording captures each app's
  sound and CoreEQ's equalized copy 27 ms later, which sounds like an echo.
  CoreEQ now steps aside while such a recording runs, says so, and comes back
  when it stops. On by default, in Settings → General. Microphone recordings
  are left alone.
- *Preset descriptions.* Rest the pointer on a built-in preset, in the sidebar
  or the menu, for a line on what it is for.

* Fixed

- *A sample rate change left the sound chopped* (#27). A DAW or a player such as
  Qobuz switching the output's rate left CoreEQ glitching until a restart. It
  now rebuilds its audio path once the new rate has settled.
- *The menu's switch looked the same on and off* (#26). It is now drawn in your
  accent colour when on.
- *Starting during a recording could claim the permission was refused.* A
  recorder holding the output open read as audio CoreEQ should have heard.
- *A tap could be built that heard CoreEQ itself*, when its own audio process
  could not be identified. It now waits and retries instead.

* Notes

The README now shows how to check for yourself that CoreEQ installs no audio
driver, and states what it does not do on purpose: no volume control, not
bit-perfect, one curve for every channel.

The echo some people hear after waking from sleep is not fixed, because it has
not been reproduced. If it happens to you, the diagnostics report now says
whether a second audio path was left behind, and that report is the fastest way
to an answer.

The full account of each change — what was measured, and what the first fix got
wrong — is in [[file:../RELEASE_NOTES.org][RELEASE_NOTES.org]], and what is still owed is in
[[file:../ROADMAP.org][ROADMAP.org]].
