#+TITLE: CoreEQ 1.10 — Leave apps out, find a correction
#+AUTHOR: Andrey Pudov
#+DATE: <2026-10-23 Fri>
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

1.10 lets you leave apps out of the equalizer, and finds an AutoEq correction
for your headphones without leaving CoreEQ. It also changes how releases are
signed, so this is the last update that asks for the audio permission again.

* Before you update

*CoreEQ asks for the audio permission once more, on this update.* Releases are
now signed with CoreEQ's own certificate, and macOS records the permission
against that certificate rather than against each build, so every later update
keeps it. This one changes the kind of signature, so macOS asks one last time.
CoreEQ now explains what the permission is for and asks from an *Allow…* button,
instead of the prompt appearing at launch.

Gatekeeper is unchanged: the certificate is not Apple's, so the first launch of
a download still needs *Open Anyway* in System Settings → Privacy & Security, or
the =xattr= step in the README.

* New

- *The AutoEq catalog.* The *AutoEq* button beside the =+=, and *Browse AutoEq
  Catalog…* in the =+= menu, search AutoEq's corrections by headphone model.
  Selecting a measurement plays it against whatever is playing, without saving
  anything; *Cancel* puts your sound back, and *Import* keeps it as a preset.
  Other compatible target curves are computed in the app. Nothing is downloaded
  until you open the catalog, and a correction heard once previews offline.
  Contributed by Sebastian Ullrich.
- *Leaving apps out* (#20). Settings → *Exclusions* lists apps whose sound goes
  straight to the output, never through CoreEQ: a DAW, a mastering chain, a
  player you want bit for bit. Each has a switch, so one can have its EQ back
  for a while without being removed. Needs macOS 26 or later. Browsers can't be
  excluded, because they play through a helper process macOS shares between
  apps.
- *Narrower bells* (#41). A parametric bell's Q now goes to 16, up from 10, for
  the sharp resonances in-ear headphones are corrected with.
- *Imports say what they change.* Free filters now reach ±20 dB, and any value
  that still has to change is listed before anything is added — the filter, the
  old and new value, and what it does to the response in dB. A bell past the
  graphic sliders' ±12 dB can be kept exact or clipped onto its slider.
- *Search that finds model numbers.* Presets and catalog models match word by
  word, ignoring case, accents and punctuation: “sony mdr m1” finds Sony
  MDR-M1, and “600” finds both the HD 600 and the HD600.
- *The editor follows the graph.* Clicking a numbered node on the graph shows
  the Parametric tab with its row selected; a round handle shows the Graphic
  tab.
- *The preamp works with AUTO on.* Dragging it takes over from AUTO;
  double-clicking returns it to the preset's value.

* Fixed

- *Two copies of CoreEQ silenced the Mac.* Each one's tap muted the other's
  output. A second copy now asks which one to quit before it starts.
- *Moving between catalog previews played your own preset in between.* A
  preview now replaces the previous one directly.
- *The band list came up empty* after switching from a long parametric preset
  to a shorter one while scrolled down.
- *A refused permission and one never asked looked the same*, and a refusal
  could take six seconds of playing audio to notice. CoreEQ now asks macOS
  directly.

* Notes

All interface text is now in one String Catalog, reviewed against a single
style guide. Nothing is translated yet; this is what a translation would start
from.

The diagnostics report now includes the AutoEq catalog's version and, if it
failed to load, why. Its description in Settings now says everything it names:
your audio devices, your presets, and any apps you've excluded.
