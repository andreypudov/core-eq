#+TITLE: CoreEQ 1.8 — Make it work everywhere it claims to
#+AUTHOR: Andrey Pudov
#+DATE: <2026-09-20 Sun>
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

CoreEQ 1.7 worked on a Mac with stereo speakers and nothing unusual attached.
1.8 is about the machines where it did not. Three separate defects could leave a
Mac completely silent while every layer of the app reported that it was working,
and the only way out was to quit an app that launches at login. All three are
fixed, multichannel devices are equalized properly rather than mangled, and the
Mac is allowed to sleep again.

Everything below was measured on hardware rather than reasoned about, and the
diagnostics report carries the evidence so the next report arrives with its own.

* New

- *Multichannel output.* Devices presenting more than two channels — HDMI,
  DisplayPort, USB interfaces — are equalized per channel and placed correctly
  instead of silenced or mangled. Confirmed on a sixteen channel device: six
  channel content arrives as six discrete channels, Flat is bit-exact, and each
  channel's measured response matches the drawn curve to within 0.01 dB.
- *The Mac sleeps again.* The audio device is released after thirty seconds of
  digital silence and rebuilt the moment something plays — 40–48 ms on a
  Bluetooth headset, measured from the player launching, so the first second of
  audio is already equalized. Settings has a switch to turn the behaviour off.
- *Permission explained where it is asked.* "System Audio Recording" sounds
  broader than what an equalizer does, so the explanation now appears inside the
  macOS dialog itself rather than in Settings after the fact.
- *A diagnostics report worth pasting.* It now carries the sample-rate window
  (or "none seen"), whether the device is currently released and how often it
  has been, and the peak output with clipping attributed to the chain rather
  than to the recording.

* Fixed

- *Aggregate and Multi-Output Devices silenced the Mac.* Core Audio will not
  nest one aggregate inside another and does not say so — it returns success and
  hands back a device with no output streams. CoreEQ now refuses such a default
  output before the tap is created, and says which device and why.
- *Refusing the audio permission silenced the Mac.* Creating a tap succeeds even
  when permission was refused; every sample is zero while every other process is
  muted at the hardware. Muting is now earned — nothing is muted until real
  audio has been seen — so a refusal costs only that audio is not equalized, and
  the app says so and opens the right Settings pane.
- *CoreEQ prevented sleep with nothing playing.* Once anything had played, the
  device was never let go. See above.
- *Clipping was blamed on the material.* The warning counted any sample over
  full scale, which fires at Flat on modern masters. It now counts only samples
  the chain itself lifted past full scale.
- *Tab skipped newly added parametric bands.* The key view loop was computed
  once when the window was built and never again.

* Notes

Several of these came from reports on Reddit and GitHub, and two were found
while chasing a third — including the aggregate device defect, which had
probably been silencing people who had no way to work out what to blame. If
something is wrong on your hardware, the diagnostics report is the fastest path:
it now answers most of these questions without a round trip.

The full account of each defect — what was measured, what the first fix got
wrong, and what is still owed — is in [[file:../RELEASE_NOTES.org][RELEASE_NOTES.org]] and
